NZSL is New Zealand's third official language — and a completely different language from English, not English with hand signs. For many of the 3,000–4,000 Deaf New Zealanders who use it as a first language, English is a second language. Government forms, health notices and council letters are often hard to follow.

Plainly is a best attempt at applying published plain-language accessibility research — not a verified or finished solution. It has not yet been tested with Deaf or NZSL-first readers.

What changed, and why

Honest limits Plain English is still English — it is not NZSL, and this tool does not close the access gap on its own. It's a small bridge, most useful to Deaf readers who already have some English literacy, not everyone. The necessary next step is putting Plainly in front of Deaf and NZSL-first readers to find out whether it's actually useful — that isn't something the team can claim on its own behalf.
